F-100 is the financial command surface issued to a Genesis Institution's designated settlement office at first settlement. It is the instrument through which a Provost, CFO, or Foundation treasurer sees exactly what the partnership pays, which departments generate it, and how to verify every dollar independently. This document specifies the surface and renders it.

§1

What it is, and who holds it

Provisioned at first settlement · one credential per institution

Most institutional dashboards are reporting surfaces: a vendor computes a number, formats it, and asks you to believe it. F-100 inverts that posture. Every figure it displays is reconstructible from the public chain without the platform's cooperation — the dashboard is a convenient view onto a ledger your auditor could rebuild from scratch if they chose to.

That distinction is the whole design brief. It is why the interface is built around forensic traceability rather than executive summary, why every settlement row carries a transaction identifier and its originating Hard-Gate, and why the archival audit control is a first-class element rather than a buried export.

The surface serves three jobs simultaneously, and the layout is organized around them: confirm what has settled (the cumulative ledger and inflow stream), attribute it to a source (departmental yield), and act on what it implies (corridor demand intelligence).

Credential holders
  • Settlement office of record — Treasurer, Foundation, or Provost discretionary fund. Full surface.
  • Institutional auditor — read plus archival audit initiation. No settlement reassignment.
  • Deans, by college — departmental yield scoped to their own unit only.
  • Faculty steering chair — corridor demand intelligence. No financial figures.
Never a holder

No credential at any tier resolves an individual student. The dashboard has no per-student view to permission — that surface does not exist in the data model.

§3

Anatomy · six modules, read left to right

Each keyed to the surface above
1

Cumulative settlement · the editorial anchor

The largest type on the page, and deliberately so. It states total settlement across both instruments — the Access Split and the Dividend Split — with each broken out beneath rather than blended into a single opaque figure. Year-over-year movement sits beside it in tertiary blue because it is a verified derived metric, not a projection.

Source · on-chain settlement events, summed · reconcilable to Merkle root
2

Next settlement block · the sovereign gradient

The only element on the surface carrying the full sovereign gradient, because it is the only forward-looking one. A live countdown to the next settlement with an estimate split by instrument. It answers the single question a treasurer opens the dashboard to ask: when does the next money arrive, and roughly how much.

Source · settlement scheduler + in-flight transaction pool
3

Alumni Performance Ledger · departmental attribution

Yield attributed to the academic unit that produced the graduate, with average AICI and activation count. Bars are proportional to the leading department rather than to an arbitrary ceiling, so relative standing is legible at a glance. Efficiency tier is a composite of yield-per-activation and AICI band — it is not a ranking of teaching quality, and the surface says so on hover.

Source · ontology node → department mapping, set by your steering committee at Stage 04
4

Inflow Stream · the forensic register

Every settlement event, newest first, carrying its transaction ID, originating Hard-Gate or fee cycle, gross transaction value, and your share. Dividend rows resolve to a single anonymized sovereign; Access rows resolve to a term-level cohort count. Row colour encodes instrument — gold for Dividend, blue for Access — so the two are never read as one series.

Source · settlement log · exportable · independently verifiable against chain
5

Actionable Intelligence · corridor demand

The only advisory element on the surface, and the only one held in tertiary blue rather than gold, because it is intelligence rather than settlement. It surfaces which ontology nodes are clearing at premium in your corridor — typically two to four quarters before placement data would show the same signal. Deans use it to direct curricular investment; it never carries a financial commitment.

Source · corridor-wide demand telemetry, aggregated across all institutions, k-anonymous
6

Global Settlement Pulse · the corridor tape

A persistent tape carrying your institution's position alongside corridor liquidity status and recent settlement events. It exists to situate the institution inside a market rather than inside its own account — the same reason a trading floor keeps a tape running when nobody is watching it directly. Honours prefers-reduced-motion.

Source · corridor event bus · institution-scoped and public-corridor rows only
§4

The dual-ledger requirement

Two instruments · same ratio · never merged in the interface

The original F-100 concept surfaced only the hire-side 40%. That is no longer sufficient: an institution receives settlement from two structurally different instruments that happen to divide on the same ratio, and a dashboard that blends them into one figure actively creates the confusion the partnership documents work to prevent. The surface therefore separates them at every level — headline, inflow row colour, settlement estimate, and export schema.

LEDGER A · THE ACCESS SPLIT

Per enrolled student, per semester

The $100 Sovereign Access Fee, assessed per enrolled student per term. Settles twice a year in two large events rather than continuously — which is why it appears in the inflow stream as a single term-level row against a cohort count, not as thousands of individual rows.

The verification path

Four steps, no cooperation required

  • Export the settlement schedule for any period from the dashboard.
  • Take each event's Merkle leaf and its block anchor.
  • Verify inclusion against the published root on Polygon mainnet.
  • Sum the verified events and compare to the dashboard headline.

If the two disagree, the chain is correct and we are wrong. That ordering is the point of the architecture — your auditor audits the ledger, not our books.

Archival audit

One control, full reconstruction

The Initiate Archival Audit control produces a complete forensic reconstruction of every settlement in a selected period, formatted for institutional audit and state reporting, cryptographically signed at generation. Your internal auditor never files a data request with Tenured AI.

§7

Design rationale

Why it looks like this and not like a SaaS dashboard
Tonal layering, no rules

Every boundary on the surface is a background shift between the three surface tiers — never a hairline. Containment reads as paper stacked on paper rather than cells in a grid, which is the difference between a ledger you trust and a spreadsheet you tolerate.

Gold is scarce and load-bearing

The sovereign gradient appears exactly twice: the next-settlement block and the leading department bar. Both are the elements the treasurer scans for first. Everywhere gold could have been decorative it was replaced with tertiary blue, which carries verified-intelligence meaning instead.

Instrument colour is semantic

Gold rows are Dividend, blue rows are Access. The colour is not styling — it is the only thing preventing a reader from summing two different instruments into a single misleading series, which is the specific failure this revision exists to close.

Mono for anything auditable

Every figure a person might reconcile is set in JetBrains Mono: transaction IDs, timestamps, shares, wallet fragments, AICI values. Editorial Sora is reserved for totals and headings. The typeface tells you whether a number is a claim or a receipt.

Diffused ambient depth

Elevation uses broad 24–40px shadows at 4–8% opacity tinted from the warm ink, never a hard drop shadow. Cards read as physical stock resting on stationery. The countdown block carries a warmer shadow derived from the gradient itself.

The tape earns its motion

The corridor tape is the only animated element, and it carries information rather than atmosphere. It respects reduced-motion preferences and freezes for print. Nothing else on the surface moves, because a settlement ledger that fidgets reads as unserious.

§8

Provisioning

When it appears and what triggers it
At MOU execution

Settlement office of record is designated. No dashboard yet — there is nothing to display and a dashboard of zeroes teaches the wrong thing.

At cohort activation

Access Split ledger opens at the first term assessment. The surface goes live with Ledger A populated and Ledger B empty.

At first Hard-Gate

Dividend Split ledger opens. Departmental attribution becomes meaningful once activations exist to attribute. Dean credentials issue here.

At first settlement

Full surface. Archival audit unlocks, corridor tape connects, auditor credential issues. This is the F-100 as specified above.
