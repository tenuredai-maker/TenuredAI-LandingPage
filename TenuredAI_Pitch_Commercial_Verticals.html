The Thesis · Why One Kernel Serves Four Industries

We do not sell AI. We sell the proof that the AI was not guessing.

Every one of these markets has already bought AI. None of them can defend it. The gap between deployment and defensibility is where the revenue is — and it is the same gap in all four, because the underlying question is identical: can you demonstrate, after the fact, that the system was operating within nominal parameters at the moment of the decision?

THE STRUCTURAL INSIGHT

A hospital, a grid operator, a trading desk and a compliance office are four different businesses. But each of them is, at the moment of an AI-mediated decision, an entity that will be asked to prove what it knew and when.

That proof is a single product. The domain adapters differ. The kernel does not. This is why four verticals do not cost four times the engineering — and why the margin structure holds as we add markets.

The commercial consequence

Risk budgets are larger and less contested than technology budgets

A hospital CIO negotiates a software line item. A hospital's captive insurer negotiates a premium. The second conversation is an order of magnitude larger, has a shorter approval path, and is measured against a loss ratio rather than a feature comparison. Every pricing instrument in this deck is designed to be paid from the second budget, not the first.

MECHANIC 01

Cascade prevention

The grid is modeled as a dynamic decay graph. Proprietary threshold calibration identifies the exact point at which a localized failure crosses into propagation, and triggers isolation or load-shed before it does — rather than after the second substation drops.

MECHANIC 02

Predictive telemetry

Substation sensors and smart meters feed coherence monitoring on the grid's digital twin. A drop in the Inference Logic channel signals physical component failure four to six hours ahead. Maintenance moves from reactive to scheduled.

MECHANIC 03

Verified reconfiguration

Every proposed reconfiguration — bringing a data center online, rerouting around maintenance — passes a necessity check proving it cannot violate thermal or phase-balance limits. The Critic agent simulates extreme weather and cyber-attack against the plan; the Auditor confirms FERC and NERC posture.

Priced against value at risk, not against seats.

The failure mode in financial AI is algorithmic drift — a system executing against a hallucination or a manipulated price feed. The Integrity Layer runs formal pre-flight verification on every proposed action, before capital moves. One prevented eight-figure event pays the annual fee many times over, which is the entire commercial argument.

MECHANIC 01

Pre-flight verification

Before execution, the proposed action is formally verified against the desk's own constraint set — slippage ceilings, liquidity confirmation across independent sources, exposure limits. The trade does not clear the gate unless the constraints are provably satisfied.

MECHANIC 02

Drift detection

Coherence monitoring on the execution model surfaces the transition from reasoning to pattern-matching — the state in which manipulated inputs get acted on. Below the coherence floor, the desk halts automatically under triple-condition termination.

MECHANIC 03

Adversarial council review

On strategy changes and parameter updates, the Critic agent runs flash-loan, oracle-manipulation and liquidity-drain scenarios against the proposal. The Auditor holds the change against the firm's own risk mandate before it reaches production.

Pricing Architecture · Three Instruments, One Principle

We never price the software. We price a share of the loss that does not occur.

Every instrument in the portfolio is denominated in the buyer's own risk currency — premium, capacity, notional, or audit cost. This is deliberate: it removes the feature comparison, moves the approval to a different committee, and makes the renewal conversation an actuarial one rather than a procurement one.

INSTRUMENT I

Risk-share

A percentage of verified, carrier-confirmed savings. Used where an insurer will underwrite the reduction — Healthcare's Safety Dividend is the reference implementation.

Properties
  • · Highest alignment · zero buyer downside
  • · Requires carrier validation to price
  • · Longest to first revenue · deepest moat once set
INSTRUMENT II

Value-share

A percentage of an asset the buyer can now use that they previously could not. Energy's Capacity Fee and Financial Integrity's Protected Value Fee both sit here.

Properties
  • · Scales with the buyer's own balance sheet
  • · Requires an agreed measurement protocol up front
  • · Expands without a renegotiation
INSTRUMENT III

Displacement fee

A fixed annual charge priced beneath a documented manual cost the buyer is already paying. The Regulatory Integrity Fee is the reference: $32K–$50K per model against a $250K–$500K manual alternative.

Properties
  • · Fastest to close · simplest to model
  • · Predictable ARR · clean renewal
  • · Most exposed to price competition long-term
What we do not sell, under any framing

Per-seat licences. Per-API-call metering. Perpetual source licences. Unbundled access to the coherence thresholds, the cascade calibration, or the modal constraint library. The trade-secret layer is never a deliverable — it is the reason the instruments above can be priced the way they are, and it does not leave the platform in any commercial structure.

The sequencing principle

Each wave produces the artifact the next wave needs. Regulatory produces the compliance packet that a hospital's general counsel will accept. The Ask · Design Partner Structure

One model. One quarter. One measurement protocol.

Every vertical enters through the same door: a single high-risk model, system, or desk, instrumented for one quarter, against a measurement protocol we agree before we start. No enterprise rollout, no platform migration, no committed spend. At the end of the quarter, the buyer has a signed evidence packet and a verified number — and the pricing conversation is arithmetic rather than argument.
