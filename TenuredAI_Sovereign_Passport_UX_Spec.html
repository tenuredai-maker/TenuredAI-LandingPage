PASSPORT v2 / F1Foundations · The Sovereign Artifact

The platform's outputs, collected into one artifact the user owns.

The Sovereign Passport is not a profile, a portfolio, or a LinkedIn page. It is the user-facing artifact where every patent-protected output of the platform becomes visible, portable, and independently verifiable. A recruiter, an underwriter, a state regulator, or the user themselves can read the Passport without trusting Tenured AI — every claim it makes is anchored to the Sovereign Ledger (PAT-010) and the agent signatures (PAT-004) that produced it. v2.0 reconciles the Passport with the patent vocabulary used everywhere else in the platform.

Strategy · the Passport is the platform's IP made personal

Every other surface in the platform produces ephemeral output — a Drill ends, a Gate closes, an Underwriting decision settles. The Passport is the only place where all of those outputs accumulate, get stamped with their patent provenance, and become something the user takes with them. This makes the Passport the platform's most strategically important surface from an IP standpoint: it's where Tenured AI's twelve patents become one credential a real person can show. If the Passport doesn't render the patent-protected provenance, the platform looks like LinkedIn-with-extra-steps. v2.0's job is to make that provenance unmissable.

Your knowledge half-life, on the Passport.

PASSPORT v2 / F9Foundations · Asymmetric Liability Surfacing (PAT-019)

Honest about overconfidence.

PAT-019's OL (Overconfidence Liability) flags surface here. When a user has cleared a Gate but the Auditor's record shows a high-confidence wrong answer on a specific node, that OL flag is attached to the Consensus Certificate — and visible to the user on the owner view, and disclosed to the recruiter in the recruiter view. The platform's defensibility depends on never hiding what the council recorded. A bonded hire with three Tier-2 OL flags is a different risk than one with zero, and the underwriter needs to see it.

Tier 1 · ADVISORY L3-L4 + wrong, isolated event Logged silently · visible to owner only
Tier 2 · CRITICAL L5 + wrong, OR pattern across 3+ events Flag on certificate · disclosed to recruiter on reveal
Tier 3 · BOND-IMPACT L5 failure on bonded competency Disclosed to underwriter · triggers OSD recalibration

PASSPORT v2 / ★Designer's Notes

Ten decisions that make v2.0 patent-complete.

The throughlines a future team must protect to keep the Passport a sovereign artifact rather than a profile page.

01

CA is the headline.

Command Authority replaces Triple-Threat as the hero metric on every audience view. AICI/AIOI/AIBS remain as supporting components. The first 200ms of perception decide the platform's authority.

02

Credentials are Consensus Certificates.

Every credential displayed has its four agent signatures, WCS, and Merkle structure visible on tap. The math is the moat; rendering the math is what makes the moat legible.

03

The Evidence Locker is browseable.

PoF records are first-class — a recruiter who reveals a credential gets the 6-channel ALTFL replay. The platform's defensibility against LLM-faked résumés depends on this surface being deep.

04

Decay is shown openly.

λ is visible on the Passport. Credentials carry their current St state. Hiding decay would be the same dishonesty as a credit score hiding which behaviors moved it.

05

Career Memory is exportable.

The Vector-Graph artifact archive belongs to the user. Export-as-JSON, export-as-PDF, takes-it-with-them-if-they-leave. The Passport is a career-long home, not a rental.

06

OL flags are disclosed.

Asymmetric liability surfacing is honest. The recruiter who shows a Triple-85 candidate with two cleared OL flags makes a more credible case than one who hides them. Transparency is the underwriting moat.

07

Bond status is on the Passport.

v2.0's most consequential integration. The Passport becomes the document a recruiter shows their CFO — V_u, π, strike margin, bonded credentials, claim history. The platform's institutional pitch lives here.

08

Redaction is the owner's right.

Every audience sees the Passport at the level the owner authorized. Recruiter reveals consume credits. Public visibility is opt-in. Privacy is the product, not a feature.

09

Verification doesn't require us.

Anyone with the DID can walk the Merkle tree on Polygon and verify any credential without Tenured AI participating. Open-source verification client provided. Trust degrades gracefully if our servers are down.

10

The Passport renders the platform's twelve patents.

CA from PAT-001, PoF from PAT-002, λ from PAT-003, Consensus Certificate from PAT-004, Career Memory from PAT-005, simulation framing from PAT-008, Merkle anchor from PAT-010, Tier evolution from PAT-011, Bond status from PAT-013/014, OL flags from PAT-019, Annotations from PAT-020 — every patent in the platform's portfolio has a visible surface on this artifact. The Passport is where the IP becomes a credential a real person owns. Future teams must preserve this principle: when a new patent ships, its output ends up on the Passport, or the patent is for a system the user can't see.
