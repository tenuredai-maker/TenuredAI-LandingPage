UW / F1Foundations · The $100B Mechanism

From talent platform to financial infrastructure.

Tenured AI doesn't just grade talent — it underwrites it. By capturing 24/7 telemetric "Proof of Friction" through the Proving Ground, the platform provides the first-ever Financial Guarantee for human performance. The three B2B surfaces in this spec are how that guarantee is bought, monitored, and claimed.

FOR THE RECRUITER

Stop hiring liabilities

The I-100 Recruiter Command Center filters every candidate through the 4-condition AIRF™ authenticity gate. You only see candidates who've proven skills in live simulations — and only after they accept your reveal request.

FOR THE CFO

Hedge the workforce

EWARD mathematically audits your team's Sovereign Density, predicts time-to-recover from disruption, identifies single points of failure, and issues underwriteable Performance Bonds your insurer will accept.

FOR THE PROVOST

Compete on outcomes

The L-200 War Room ranks universities by Gross Dividend Asset (GDA) — real dollars returned via the 40/40/20 split — instead of rankings prestige. Compete on graduates' verified market valuation, not on a magazine list.

Strategy · why three surfaces are one product

These look like three products but they are one underwriting machine. Recruiters buy access to verified talent; enterprises buy bonds against that talent's failure; institutions earn dividends when their graduates are hired and bonded. Each surface feeds the others — the recruiter's reveal credit funds the dividend that ranks the institution that produced the candidate the enterprise just underwrote. Designing them as one specification (with a shared underwriting math, a shared Corridor Pulse, a shared Sovereign Passport feed) is what makes the three-way flywheel legible to anyone reviewing the build.

Designer's Note · separate surfaces, shared spine

It would be tempting to merge these into "a B2B dashboard with three tabs." That would be the wrong move. Each audience operates under different time pressure (recruiter: minutes; enterprise: quarters; institution: years), and a single tabbed UI would punish the recruiter's speed and underserve the institution's depth. So we design three independent surfaces sharing a common data spine (Corridor Pulse, Passport feed, underwriting math) but presenting completely different control rooms. That's how an underwriting bank actually works: one bond ledger, many product desks.

UW / F3Foundations · AIRF™ 5-Tier Authenticity Gate

Four conditions. Five tiers. Zero "flashcard engineers."

The AIRF™ Classifier (PAT-013, Module 1352) is the platform's filter against false signal. A candidate earns a tier only when all four conditions clear simultaneously: Verified Count, Accuracy, Calibration Gap, and Overconfident Liability count. This is what lets a recruiter mathematically reject the arrogant-but-unproven candidate that LinkedIn would surface.

TIER 1
Below Threshold

Insufficient signal — recruiters do not see by default.

TIER 2
Familiar

Some verification, calibration not yet trustworthy.

TIER 3
Working Knowledge

Recruiter-visible. Reliable in standard scenarios.

TIER 4
Verified Practitioner

High Verified Count + low OL. Bond-eligible.

TIER 5
Sovereign Practitioner

All conditions clear. Triple-85 candidate.

THE 4-CONDITION GATE — ALL MUST CLEAR

Verified CountNumber of Hard-Gate / Lab verifications across cross-surface attribution.Volume of evidence — a single Gate is a sample, not a track record.
AccuracyProportion of verifications that passed without override.Quality — high-volume + low-accuracy is noise.
Calibration GapDifference between self-rated and verified competency.Honesty signal — high gap = overconfidence.

Strategy · the four-condition gate is the moat

Most "skill scores" can be gamed by volume — answer enough questions, the score goes up. The AIRF™ gate is mathematically impossible to game because the four conditions conflict: high Verified Count without Calibration discipline produces OL events that cap your tier. The honest, careful candidate clears all four; the showboat clears one or two and gets capped at Tier 1 or 2. Surfacing this gate clearly in the recruiter UI (with the OL count visible per candidate) is what converts "Tenured AI grades skills" into "Tenured AI mathematically filters liabilities" — a much stronger product claim.

UW / F4Foundations · 3-Phase Privacy-Preserving Match Protocol

Recruiters pay to reach. Candidates choose to be reached.

PAT-013 Module 1354 inverts the power dynamic of every existing talent platform. The recruiter sees a match count, then de-identified profiles, then — and only after paying a credit and the candidate accepting — the identity. Top talent will pay nothing to participate; recruiters will pay premium SaaS fees to reach them.

PHASE 1

Match Count

Recruiter runs a Boolean query. Result: "42 matches at Tier 4+ in Houston-Energy corridor." Zero identifying information. Free.

PHASE 2

De-Identified Profiles

"Candidate c0001 · Tier 5 · 7 Lab Verifications · AIBS 87." Tiers, scores, attribution sources visible. No name, no contact, no employer. Included with base subscription.

PHASE 3

Opt-In Reveal

Recruiter spends a Reveal Credit + submits a structured Hire Brief. Candidate reviews recruiter, role, comp. Only if they accept is identity disclosed. The credit is consumed regardless.

Designer's Note · friction is the feature

Every existing recruiter platform optimizes for recruiter ease — fast contact, mass outreach, easy InMails. This is precisely why senior engineers hate them. We do the opposite: deliberately add friction to recruiter contact (credits + structured brief + opt-in), which selects for serious specific opportunities and protects the candidate's attention. That asymmetric cost — cheap to be a candidate, expensive to be a recruiter — is what attracts the talent pool LinkedIn cannot reach.

Two simultaneous conditions trigger payout from the Underwriting Reserve. Documented in the Strike sequence (E6).

Strategy · the math is the moat, render it as the moat

These formulas are the platform's defensible IP. Showing them clearly in the EWARD UI — not as decoration but as the actual underwriting display — is a deliberate trust move: the CFO who sees π = Base × (λ/CLI) on their dashboard understands exactly what they're paying for and why. Hiding the math would make this look like another vendor's black-box SaaS; rendering it makes it look like a Bloomberg terminal. We use the inverse-vault surface, mono type, and amber CRT glow specifically to make these formulas feel like instrumentation.

UW / F6Foundations · OSD & TTR_org

The two numbers a CFO actually wants.

Organizational Sovereign Density (OSD) is the percentage of an enterprise's workforce verified at Tier 4+ in the skills the enterprise actually depends on. Time-to-Recovery (TTR_org) is how fast that organization can recover from a defined AI disruption, projected from the verified Growth Coefficients of its staff. These two numbers run the EWARD dashboard.

OSD · Organizational Sovereign Density
OSD = Σ (StN × wN × σN) / Wc_total

Cohort-level Sovereign Density per node domain. Surfaces "Absolute Gaps" (no one cleared), "Partial Gaps" (insufficient depth), "Prospective Gaps" (decaying), and "Prerequisite Chain" gaps (downstream dependency holes).

TTR_org · Time-to-Recovery
TTR_org = Gapδ / mean(γcohort × (1/λsim))

The Chaos Scenario Simulator injects a disruption (e.g., "Vector DB ecosystem shift"), recomputes cohort λ, and projects how many weeks the org needs to restore OSD. The number a CRO loses sleep over.

Designer's Note · the simulator's job is to scare the right CFO

This screen has one job: convert "we should probably train our team on AI" into "we have a $1.2M bond exposure if EU AI Act enforcement accelerates and we need to act this quarter." The mono inverse-vault projection card is intentionally severe — error-red TTR deltas, hard dollar exposure — because the CFO who needs this surface should feel the urgency the numbers actually warrant. Soft-pedaling the projection would be the same dishonesty as a credit agency rounding a rating up.

Designer's Note · the Gavel is the most consequential action in the platform

Releasing $150K from the Reserve based on evidence is the closest the platform comes to an actual courtroom moment. We use the literal gavel verb — not "Process Payout" or "Release Funds" — because the action deserves its weight. The evidence chamber sits visible right above it, the dispute escape hatch is always available, and the payout animation is deliberately slow. This single screen is what makes the entire underwriting story credible: when a strike happens, the platform actually pays, and the CFO sees the receipt hash that proves it.

Candidate withdrew

The Recruiter Battle is everything Tenured AI argues, compressed into 90 seconds: a verified Tier-5 candidate, multiple serious recruiters paying real money, a live institutional dividend rising on screen, snipe protection borrowed from professional procurement, atomic settlement to a bond. We render this with the most aggressive financial-terminal grammar in the entire system — pulsing red snipe window, inverse vault top-bid, mono everywhere — because this is the surface that converts the whole story into a single visceral moment. Anyone seeing this auction live understands the business model.

UW / AAccessibility

Financial infrastructure must be readable by every operator.

EWARD and L-200 are operated by executives, treasurers, auditors, and legislators — many over 50, many with mature reading-accommodation needs. The underwriting math must be readable, the heatmaps narratable, the auction operable from a keyboard.

WCAG 2.2 AA

  • · Mono finance numerals use ≥14px on screens; "$" prefix and "%" suffix mandatory so screen readers parse currency/percent correctly.
  • · Heatmap cells always carry both the color band AND the numeric coverage (00, 12, 38, 64, 88) — never color-only.
  • · Reduce Motion: count-up animations (R3, R6, E1) degrade to instant final values; auction snipe flash becomes a static color.
  • · Targets: Quick-Bid chips, gavel, intervention buttons all ≥ 44×44pt. Keyboard equivalents for every gesture.
  • · Contrast: mono amber on inverse vault validated at 4.8:1; error reds on error-container validated at 5.2:1.

VoiceOver & Dynamic Type

  • · Heatmaps expose a navigable data table fallback ("Vector DB latency, 0% coverage, Absolute Gap, est cost $84K, est ETA 6 weeks") so a non-visual user reads the org's risk register as a list.
  • · Auction (B-300) announces every bid via aria-live polite with the new top bid + dividend update, so a blind recruiter can compete in real-time.
  • · Strike Event (E6) announces the gavel commit as "Strike payout committed, $150,000 released, receipt hash 0x77ab" — auditable by ear.
  • · Dynamic Type scales to AX5; the financial tables reflow column-by-column rather than truncating mono numerals.
  • · k-anonymity floors announce explicitly when triggered ("Below privacy floor — cohort hidden") rather than silently skipping a cell.
UW / ★Designer's Notes

Eight decisions that bind the three surfaces.

The throughlines a future team must preserve to keep the I-100, EWARD, and L-200 reading as one underwriting machine rather than three loosely-related dashboards.

01

Three surfaces, one Corridor Pulse.

Every screen reads against the same shared Pulse feed. Recruiters, CFOs, and provosts see consistent market data because they are operating in one market.

02

Friction is a feature, not a bug.

Reveal credits, structured Hire Briefs, atomic bond commits, deliberate gavel — every consequential action is rendered with weight. Speed is for the recruiter; gravity is for the bond.

03

Show the math, render the moat.

π, V_u, OSD, TTR_org, GDA are all surfaced as readable formulas on inverse vault surfaces. The patents are the moat; rendering them as instrumentation is what makes that moat legible.

04

Privacy as power, k-anonymity as law.

Phase 1/2/3 protocol inverts recruiter-candidate power. OSD heatmap enforces k-anonymity even when a CFO wants to drill deeper. Privacy is the product.

05

Failure is paid, not hidden.

The Strike Event renders with full evidence + a literal gavel + a real wire of $150K. A platform that pays out builds trust no marketing copy can match.

06

Rank on dollars, not prestige.

L-200 League Table uses GDA (real dividend dollars) as #1 metric. Universities can argue with editors; they cannot argue with their own bank deposits.

07

The State is a customer too.

The Kill-Switch Dashboard treats state legislators as third audience — their 20% revenue share needs an integrity monitor. This reframes the platform as a public good.

08

The Recruiter Battle is the entire platform in 90 seconds.

Verified Tier-5 candidate, multiple serious bidders, live institutional dividend, snipe protection, atomic settlement to a bond. Anyone who watches the auction once understands the business model — which is why this surface gets the most aggressive financial grammar in the entire system. It is the surface that sells the platform to investors, regulators, and rival firms in a single screen.
